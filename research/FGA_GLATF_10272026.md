## Executive Summary

Global Atomic is a Niger-jurisdiction (high-risk) uranium developer building Dasa, a high-grade underground mine (80% owned through SOMIDA), and holds 49% of a producing Turkish zinc-recycling JV [1][2]. Commissioning is scheduled for H2 2028, and the remaining project cost is US$550.1M, of which US$433.5M is direct capital [1][3]. The Durrett 10-factor score is **4.6 / 10**.

Our sum of the parts (1.3x AISC, 0.80 development-and-construction stage factor applied to operating NAV and remaining initial capital, 8% discount rate, gross fully diluted shares) gives **$0.86, $1.23 and $2.43 per share** at U3O8 of $85, $100 and $150, against a price near **$0.24** [5]. The $100 base case sits above the analyst average target of about US$1.06 (C$1.50, five analysts, secondary source) [6]. The main drags are: (1) a funding gap, with about US$59M of pro forma cash against a US$152.7M equity requirement before the DFC loan can be drawn [3]; (2) Niger sovereign and export-route risk; (3) heavy dilution, with 958.8M gross fully diluted shares and DFC warrants not yet counted [3][4].

The first cash flow is 2.75 years from today (start 2.25 years, H2 2028 commissioning plus ramp, plus 0.5 year mid-year timing), discounted at **8%**, then multiplied by the **0.80** stage factor. Remaining initial capital is deducted using the same 0.80 stage factor, spread over the two years before start with midpoint discounting (0.25 and 1.25 years). ## Red Flags and Investor Concerns

- **Red Flag: Technology / execution risk.** Dasa is unbuilt. Direct capital rose from US$375.6M (2024 study) to US$653.0M, total cost is US$777.6M, and first production slipped from 2025 to H2 2028. Evidence for: US$227.5M spent and underground development ongoing. Evidence against: no commissioning date is fixed and DFC drawdown is conditional [1][3][9].
- **Red Flag: Location (1).** Niger's military government took power in 2023; the Benin border used for uranium exports closed in mid-2023 with only an agreement in principle to reopen, and an Algeria corridor is unsigned. DFC conditions include a viable export route, an extended mining convention and permit, and assurances on repayments [3][7][10].
- **Red Flag: Cash / Debt (2).** US$550.1M remains to be spent; US$152.7M of equity must come before DFC funds flow, against about US$59M pro forma cash [3][4][7].
- **Red Flag: Good Buzz / Chart (2).** Shares trade near their 52-week low (about $0.23) after a discounted offering, down from a $0.74 high [5][11].
- **Red Flag: Share Structure (3).** Basic shares grew from about 408M to 604M in 2026 through raises at C$0.88 and C$0.50; DFC will also receive warrants [4][7].
- **Red Flag: People / Management (4).** Repeated schedule slips and a proposed class action alleging misleading financing disclosure from Nov 2023 to Jan 2025 [9].
- **Red Flag: Cost / Financing (5).** A strong AISC is offset by a 74% direct-capital overrun and a conditional loan [2][3].

### Observations and Next Steps

Watch for: DFC definitive documents and condition satisfaction; a signed export route (Benin reopening or Algeria corridor); mining convention extension; Q3 2026 results (mid-November) for cash, debt and spend; any minority-interest sale. Verify: long-term debt and lease balances, warrant exercise prices, the fully diluted count in the Q2 MD&A share-data note, the AISC decomposition (tax and initial capital), and the Turkish JV carrying value.
