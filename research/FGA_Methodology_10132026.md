# How We Value Uranium Mining Companies
## Fifth Generation Advisers — Methodology Guide

Effective date: October 13, 2026 (supersedes the October 9, 2026 version and the earlier October 13 draft; revised for uranium-specific resource weighting, stage-weighted capital, and balance-sheet and convertible-note treatment)

Our uranium company reviews combine a Don Durrett-inspired, equal-weighted 10-factor scorecard with a project-by-project sum-of-the-parts valuation. The scorecard evaluates business quality and risk. The valuation estimates what the specified assets could be worth under flat uranium-price scenarios—not what their shares will trade at.

Method adapted from How to Invest In Gold and Silver - D. Durrett: https://a.co/d/01XVxUfE

The uranium assumptions and adjustments below are Fifth Generation Advisers' application of the framework, not a claim that Don Durrett endorses our analysis.

### Revision note (October 13, 2026)

1. Section 1 applies a separate 0.83 weight to measured and indicated resources when a project reports no proven and probable reserve. Projects with reserves continue to use 0.90 for reserves, 0.65 for measured and indicated resources excluding reserves, and 0.30 for inferred. Reports issued before October 13, 2026 used 0.65 for all non-reserve M&I and must be rerun before their plausible pounds and valuations are comparable.
2. Section 5: remaining initial capital now receives the same stage factor as the project's operating NAV. Earlier drafts deducted 100% of remaining capital. Full-capital results are shown as a required sensitivity.
3. Section 2: AISC is measured excluding initial capital (deducted separately) and excluding income tax (covered by the 30% pad). Fully loaded company or study costs must be decomposed before use.
4. Section 5: uranium inventory is marked to the scenario price; convertible notes are deducted at face value, with capped calls considered before adding conversion shares.
5. Stage factors, discount rate and cost pad are unchanged.

## 1. Start with attributable, plausible pounds

We distinguish proven and probable reserves, measured and indicated resources, and inferred resources. Reserves have demonstrated economic support; resources do not necessarily have demonstrated economic viability. Inferred resources have lower geological confidence.

Our confidence weights are:

- Proven and probable reserves: 90%.
- Measured and indicated resources, inclusive of reserves, where no proven and probable reserve is reported: 83%.
- Measured and indicated resources excluding reserves, where a reserve is reported: 65%.
- Inferred resources: 30%.

The 0.83 weight is used only for projects with no reported proven and probable reserve. It is never applied in addition to the 0.65 weight for the same pounds, and it is never applied to the portion of M&I that overlaps reported reserves.

**Projects with a reported reserve** (resources reported inclusive of reserves):

Plausible pounds = 0.90 x P&P + 0.65 x (M&I - P&P) + 0.30 x inferred.

**Projects with no reported reserve:**

Plausible pounds = 0.83 x M&I + 0.30 x inferred.

Attributable plausible pounds = plausible pounds x ownership percentage.

When a source reports resources exclusive of reserves, we use those exclusive figures directly as the M&I-excluding-reserves term; we do not subtract reserves a second time. We verify category definitions, effective dates and whether figures are on a 100%-project or attributable basis. Contained resources and recoverable reserves may use different technical bases, so subtraction can be an approximation and must be disclosed. Every report must state when the no-reserve 0.83 weight was applied.

### Example 1: project with a reserve

Using the Rook I inputs in our review: 239.6 million reserve pounds, 256.7 million M&I pounds inclusive of reserves, and 80.7 million inferred pounds:

- Reserves: 239.6 x 0.90 = 215.640 million pounds.
- M&I excluding reserves: (256.7 - 239.6) x 0.65 = 11.115 million pounds.
- Inferred: 80.7 x 0.30 = 24.210 million pounds.
- Total: 250.965 million pounds, rounded to 251.0 million.

At 100% ownership, attributable plausible pounds also equal 251.0 million.

### Example 2: project with no reserve

Using the Dewey-Burdock inputs in our enCore review: 17.122 million M&I pounds and 0.713 million inferred pounds, with no reported reserve:

- M&I: 17.122 x 0.83 = 14.211 million pounds.
- Inferred: 0.713 x 0.30 = 0.214 million pounds.
- Total: 14.425 million pounds, at 100% ownership.

Under the previous rule the same project would have given 17.122 x 0.65 + 0.713 x 0.30 = 11.343 million pounds.

"Plausible pounds" is our analytical construct. It is not an NI 43-101 or SEC S-K 1300 resource category, a qualified-person estimate, or a literal statistically validated probability of recovery. The 0.83 weight is a modeling choice that gives more credit to measured and indicated resources than to inferred resources when no reserve exists; it does not mean 83% of those pounds will be recovered or are economic. Weighting pounds does not replace a recovery model or mine plan.

## 2. Use flat uranium-price scenarios and padded costs

Our default U3O8 prices are $85, $100 and $150 per pound, held flat throughout the modeled mine life. These are client-selected scenarios, not forecasts or price targets.

Margin per pound = scenario uranium price - 1.30 x AISC.

AISC means all-in sustaining cost. We prefer project-specific technical-study estimates. If unavailable, we may construct a proxy from reported operating costs, sustaining capital and other identified costs, explicitly labeling assumptions and exclusions.

**Cost basis.** Because remaining initial capital is deducted separately (Section 5) and the 30% pad already includes an allowance for tax, the AISC used in the margin excludes initial capital and excludes income tax. Fully loaded after-tax costs reported in studies must be decomposed before use. Example: the enCore Mesteña Grande PEA reports a fully loaded cost of $53.18 per pound, made up of operating cost $25.49, ad valorem tax $0.30, royalty $3.60, initial capital $12.97 and federal income tax $10.82. We use $29.39 (operating cost, ad valorem tax and royalty) and deduct the initial capital separately. Using the fully loaded figure would count capital and tax twice.

The 30% cost pad includes an allowance for tax and cost uncertainty. It is not a statutory tax calculation and does not guarantee that every expense is covered. Corporate overhead, royalties, closure costs, recovery losses and inflation must be checked separately for inclusion and duplication.

A study estimate, a company-reported production cost and a constructed AISC proxy are not interchangeable. The reports distinguish them.

## 3. Apply project-stage factors

We multiply operating cash-flow present value, and remaining initial capital (Section 5), by a development-stage factor:

- Concept or grassroots exploration: 0.20.
- Discovery: 0.30.
- Resource definition or preliminary economic assessment: 0.40.
- Completed pre-feasibility or feasibility study: 0.60.
- Development and construction: 0.80.
- Production during ramp-up: 1.00.
- Steady-state production: 1.10, where specifically justified.
- Depletion or closure: 0.80, where applicable.

For these reviews, the PRE_PFS database status means a project before a completed pre-feasibility study and receives 0.40. It should not be confused with a completed PFS, which ordinarily receives 0.60. An exploration-stage property whose resources are mostly inferred and whose only study is a PEA (for example enCore's Mesteña Grande) receives 0.40, not a development-stage factor.

Dewey-Burdock is an explicit analyst/client override at 0.60. That treatment does not establish that it has a completed PFS or a 60% probability of starting on time. Gas Hills remains at 0.40.

Stage factors are valuation adjustments, not calibrated success probabilities. The resource weights and the stage factors are applied together and each haircut should be disclosed; together they can still leave early-stage projects with substantial modeled value. We do not automatically multiply an additional permitting probability into NAV: doing so could count the same risk twice. Separate probability-weighted scenarios require explicit assumptions about success, failure, delay and capital spending.

## 4. Discount cash flows at 5%, using mid-year timing

The simplified model spreads attributable plausible pounds evenly over a stated mine life. For each project:

Project NAV = stage factor x sum of annual discounted cash flows.

Annual cash flow = (attributable plausible pounds / mine life) x (uranium price - 1.30 x AISC).

Cash-flow timing = years until production starts + production-year number - 0.5.

Discounted annual cash flow = annual cash flow / (1.05 raised to the power of cash-flow timing).

The first year's cash flow occurs at start delay plus 0.5 years. This is the mid-year convention.

### Worked example

Using 251.0 million pounds, an 11-year life, a 3.7-year start delay, $12.31 AISC and a 0.80 stage factor:

- Annual modeled pounds: 22.818 million.
- Padded cost: $16.003 per pound.
- Margin at $100 uranium: $83.997 per pound.
- Annual modeled cash flow: approximately $1,916.7 million.
- First cash flow: 4.2 years from the valuation date.
- Cash-flow present value before the stage adjustment: approximately $13,619.2 million.
- Stage-weighted operating NAV: approximately $10,895.4 million.

This is a simplified scenario, not the company's engineering-study NPV. Its plausible-pound basis, production profile, costs and timing may differ materially from a technical mine plan.

The 5% discount rate is a client instruction, not a company-specific cost of capital. A low rate can raise valuations substantially. Stage haircuts and cost padding do not demonstrate that 5% adequately prices all financing, execution or sovereign risks.

## 5. Build the equity-value bridge

Enterprise value (SOTP) = sum of project NAVs - stage-weighted present value of remaining initial capital.

Equity value = enterprise value - debt at face value and other separately modeled obligations + cash + separately valued investments and other assets (including uranium inventory marked to the scenario price).

Scenario value per share = equity value / consistently calculated diluted shares.

We check cash, restricted cash, securities, uranium inventory, receivables, property and other hard assets, debt, leases, product loans, partner financing and reclamation obligations. Material unquantified items are disclosed rather than silently assumed absent, including corporate overhead and cash burn, which the simplified model does not deduct.

### Remaining initial capital

Future initial capital is deducted separately, discounted at 5% and multiplied by the same stage factor applied to the project's operating NAV. This keeps the capital deduction on the same risk-adjusted basis as the cash flows it enables. A stage factor is a valuation haircut, not a probability, and a project that proceeds would bear the full capital; for that reason every report must also show the value with 100% of remaining capital deducted as a sensitivity.

Sustaining or wellfield capital already included in AISC is not deducted again. We separate total life-of-mine capital from initial capital, identify remaining versus historical expenditure, and disclose assumed spending dates. Default timing: remaining initial capital is spread equally over the two years before the stated production start, discounted at the midpoint of each year, so the capital window ends one-half year before the start date. Producing projects use an explicit stated schedule.

Money already spent is not deducted again from current cash and remaining project value. The reports may conservatively use a full budget when remaining spend is unavailable; that approximation must be labeled. A client-directed hypothetical capital assumption must be labeled as such and must not be described as verified remaining capital.

### Inventory and investments

Uranium inventory on hand is valued at the scenario price per pound, not at book cost, and the report shows the pounds, the book cost and the marked value. The mark is gross of selling costs and taxes, and may overstate value where pounds are committed to fixed-price contracts. Inventory is already produced and therefore is not part of in-ground resource pounds. Listed securities are valued at market, excluding any shares already distributed to shareholders.

### Shares and convertibles

We start with basic shares and identify options, warrants, RSUs, PSUs and convertible instruments. We distinguish a fully diluted potential-share count from the accounting diluted weighted-average share count.

Convertible notes are treated one of two ways, never both. If modeled as debt, the principal (face) amount, not the accounting carrying value, is deducted and the conversion shares are not added. If modeled as converted, the shares enter the denominator and the debt is removed from the numerator. Coupons are not deducted separately in the unlevered NAV.

Where a capped call or similar hedge exists, dilution between the conversion price and the cap price is offset; while the modeled per-share value is below the cap, the notes are treated as debt at face with no added shares. The capped call asset is not separately valued unless stated. Above the cap, the as-converted treatment applies, and the report must say which case is used.

Exercise proceeds are credited only when the chosen dilution convention calls for them; gross dilution without proceeds is disclosed as conservative.

Some existing company reviews retain provisional share counts or simplified debt treatments. This guide describes the intended consistent treatment; it does not certify that every historical report has resolved those limitations.

## 6. Separate asset quality from jurisdiction

A strong country does not automatically make a strong mine. We distinguish asset tier—scale, grade, economics, infrastructure and execution quality—from jurisdiction tier—legal stability, policy, permitting and sovereign risk.

Current analyst classifications for these reviews are:

- NexGen: Tier 1 asset, Tier 1 jurisdiction.
- Paladin: Tier 2 assets; Canada and Namibia are assessed separately.
- Uranium Energy: Tier 2-3 assets, Tier 1 jurisdictions.
- enCore: Tier 3 assets, Tier 1 jurisdiction.

These are analyst judgments, not formal mineral-resource categories. Canada and the United States are treated as Tier 1 jurisdictions; Namibia is treated as Tier 2 in these reviews.

Jurisdiction influences the Location score and the discussion of permits, financing and execution. We do not apply an automatic additional country multiplier to SOTP unless the report explicitly specifies one. A Tier 1 jurisdiction does not remove the possibility of litigation, permit denial or delays.

## 7. Leave unsupported exploration and growth optionality unvalued

We do not assign speculative dollar amounts to future discoveries, unquantified extensions, undeveloped prospects without defensible economics or corporate plans that cannot yet be modeled.

"Valued at zero" means excluded from the quantified base case—not worthless.

We distinguish three categories:

- Modeled assets: sufficient resource, ownership, cost and timing information to run a disclosed scenario.
- Separately valued assets: cash, listed securities, inventory or other assets with an independently supportable value, without double counting project cash flows.
- Unmodeled optionality: potential future value that cannot yet be supported quantitatively.

An excluded asset may have a purchase price, infrastructure value or strategic importance. Acquisition cost is not a guaranteed valuation floor. Historical or non-compliant resource estimates do not automatically enter the plausible-pound model, including under the 0.83 no-reserve rule; illustrative sensitivities must clearly identify their limitations.

Zero-valuing optionality can understate total value. Conversely, excluding corporate overhead, unresolved liabilities or development costs can overstate it. We disclose both directions of bias.

## 8. Explain conservatism without overstating it

The model has conservative features: resource-category haircuts, stage haircuts for developers, a 30% cost pad, no assumed discoveries and no uranium-price escalation.

However, we do not describe every scenario—or the resulting valuation—as inherently conservative. $150 uranium is an upside case. A flat price is not automatically conservative, and the 5% discount rate, simplified production profile and omission of some overhead can raise modeled value. Giving 0.83 credit to M&I resources at projects without reserves raises modeled pounds relative to the earlier 0.65 treatment, and stage-weighting remaining capital reduces the capital deduction relative to a full-capital deduction.

Any comparison with current spot or term prices must use dated sources and distinguish spot prices, long-term contracts and realized company prices. We do not claim that $85 or $100 is below market or a conservative forecast without that evidence.

## 9. Score ten equally weighted factors

Each factor receives a score from 1 to 10:

1. Properties and ownership.
2. People and management.
3. Share structure.
4. Location.
5. Projected growth.
6. Good buzz and chart.
7. Cost and financing.
8. Cash and debt.
9. Low valuation estimate.
10. Upside potential.

The total is the simple average. Scores are judgments supported by disclosed metrics, not statistical probabilities or forecasts. Original gold-oriented valuation thresholds are not mechanically applied to uranium pounds.

All factors below 6 should be identified as concerns. The radar chart summarizes the pattern; it does not replace the detailed analysis. The overall score and SOTP answer different questions and need not move together.

## 10. Source, reconcile and refresh inputs

Our preferred source order is original technical reports and regulatory filings, official company releases and presentations, regulatory permitting records, and then credible secondary sources for context or cross-checks.

For each material input we record the source, effective or financial-statement date, units, ownership basis, currency and whether it is verified, provisional or assumed.

We cross-check technical-report resources against annual filings, investigate material differences, and reconcile share counts and subsequent financings. We distinguish resource-estimate dates from report publication dates. We confirm whether a project reports a proven and probable reserve before applying the 0.83 no-reserve weight, and we retain the source showing that no reserve is reported. We also confirm the basis (loaded or unloaded) of every cost input before using it as AISC.

The consolidated project RAG JSON is a reference database, not a substitute for primary evidence. Each record's last_updated date is checked against newer information. New findings, unresolved conflicts and proposed changes are flagged; an old technical input is not made current simply by changing its update timestamp.

Older technical reports are flagged, but age alone does not establish that a report is invalid. We investigate depletion, new drilling, changed costs and permitting updates. Missing inputs are not fabricated.

Analyst targets are secondary cross-checks. Multiple aggregators may cover the same analysts, so averaging their averages is not an independent consensus estimate.

## 11. Test assumptions with sensitivities

At the $100 uranium case we test one change at a time: full (unweighted) remaining capital, lower project NAV, higher costs or initial capital, later production, additional equity financing, corporate overhead or excluded assets. For projects using the no-reserve weight, a sensitivity should also show the result using the 0.65 weight so readers can see the effect of this assumption.

An equity-raise illustration adds proceeds and new shares consistently. It is not a forecast that the company will issue at that price, incur no fees or retain all the cash.

Delay cases state whether capital spending also moves. Delaying revenue while holding capital timing fixed differs from delaying the whole project. For permitting failure, we assess both forgone cash flows and avoidable or stranded capital; simply removing gross NAV does not necessarily capture the net loss.

Capital-raise probabilities and project-start probabilities are analyst judgments unless a documented calibration method is provided. They should not be presented as actuarial estimates.

## 12. Disclose limitations and confidence

Reports identify secondary-source inputs, assumptions, missing values and unresolved balances. A Data Quality Score, where provided, is an internal self-assessment, not an independent audit or assurance opinion. Component definitions must be consistent across reports; scores built under different definitions should not be treated as directly comparable.

Principal limitations include resource and recovery uncertainty, simplified production, study-cost age, financing dilution, contract prices versus spot assumptions, permitting risk, currency effects, unresolved liabilities, corporate overhead not deducted, unmodeled assets, stage-weighted capital, and the judgmental 0.83 resource weight used where no reserve is reported.
